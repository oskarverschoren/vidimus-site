VIDIMUS/CASE FILES/WEB PAGES & PUBLICATIONS

It was online. Now it is not.

A price in a web shop, general terms and conditions, an announcement or a promise on a partner's site. A few weeks later the page has been changed or is gone, and then you hear: "that was never there." This page sets out what you can do today, what the law says, and what Vidimus does and does not record.

A customer disputes an invoice: "Your site said €49, not €59." Your own site shows €59 today. You do not have a screenshot from back then, and a screenshot you take now proves nothing about back then. What was on the page on 3 September at 14:02 (UTC+2)?

01 / THE PROBLEM

Why a screenshot so often falls short.

A

An image is quickly changed

A screenshot is an image on your own device. Changing a price or date in it takes a minute, and the counterparty knows that too.

B

The time comes from you

The date in the file name or in the corner of the screen comes from your own computer. Nothing shows that the image already existed at that time.

C

The page itself is gone

A web page changes without a trace. An archive service may have it, or may not, and certainly not at the moment you need it.

02 / TODAY

What you can already do now, even without Vidimus.

  • Save the page itself (in the browser: Save as, complete page) and take a screenshot that shows the address and the clock.
  • Email them to yourself or to the counterparty the same day. An email with an attachment on an external server is a witness you do not control yourself.
  • Check whether a public web archive kept the page on that date, and note the address of that copy.
  • If a lot is at stake, ask for a bailiff's report (a formal record of the facts drawn up by a court bailiff). It has stronger statutory evidential value than what you or Vidimus record yourselves; Vidimus does not replace it.
03 / THE LAW

What the law says.

Art. 8.11 of the Belgian Civil Code

Between businesses, there is freedom of evidence: a screenshot, a saved page or a record are admissible, and the court weighs them. The less they depend on one party, the more weight they usually carry.

eIDAS, Art. 41(2)

For a qualified electronic timestamp, there is a presumption throughout the EU that the date and time are accurate and that the data have not changed since. At Vidimus, that timestamp is on the group your record is in (for customer organisations, from GLOBALTRUST); whether the presumption also applies to your record is for the court to assess.

against a consumer

Against a private individual, stricter rules of evidence apply. There, a recorded page remains an indication, not automatically conclusive.

General information, not legal advice. Vidimus shows what was recorded and when; who is right is for the court to decide.

04 / RECORDED

What Vidimus records.

  • You enter an address, in the web app when recording (the option "Or a web page", in the Dutch app "Of een webpagina") or from your own software through the API. Our server fetches the page itself at that moment, not your computer.
  • What is recorded: the HTML as our server received it, byte for byte; the response headers, the redirects and the TLS certificate of the site; and a screenshot of the top part of the page (1280 × 2400 pixels) together with the rendered page.
  • All of it is one ordinary record: fingerprint, signature on your device, anchored within five minutes with a timestamp and afterwards also in Bitcoin, with an evidence bundle that the counterparty recomputes itself.

05 / LIMITS

What it does not prove.

  • What every visitor saw. The record shows what that address served to our server. Another visitor may have seen something different (country, language, login, personalisation). This is also stated in every record.
  • Pages behind a login. Our server does not log in anywhere; it does not record what is only visible after logging in.
  • The past. Vidimus records from the moment you ask. It can no longer fetch what was online yesterday.
  • Every site. Internal addresses, unusual ports and sites with an untrusted certificate are refused. Per organisation, at most five pages per minute can be recorded.
  • A confidential logbook. Vidimus cannot read anything there, so it cannot fetch a page for you either. You record web pages in a regular logbook.

06 / QUESTIONS

FAQ.

Is an ordinary screenshot not proof?

It is admissible evidence, but it is thin: an image on your own device, with a time from your own clock. A page fetched by a server outside your company, with the HTML, the headers and a timestamp from a service that is neither yours nor ours, usually carries more weight.

Can I record a page behind a login?

No. Our server only fetches publicly accessible pages and does not log in anywhere. You can, however, record what you see after logging in as a document or photo in a record; that then proves when you recorded it, not where it came from.

Does the counterparty see exactly what I saw?

Not necessarily. The record shows what that address served to our server, from our server in the EU. Someone looking from another country, in another language or logged in may see something different. This is stated in every record.

Can I automate this, for example my own terms every day?

Yes, through the API: your own system sends the address whenever you want. Each recording is one record.
