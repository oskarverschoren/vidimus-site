VIDIMUS/CASE FILES/CONSTRUCTION & CONTRACTORS

Extra work carried out, not paid.

The classic construction dispute: extra foundation work, a moved wall, heavier beams — approved by phone or on site, carried out properly, and at the final account: "we never asked for that." This page sets out what you can do today, what the law says, and what changes when the agreement is fixed at the moment itself and can no longer be changed.

Block B, foundation slab. The client: "Go ahead with that extra reinforcement, it will be fine." Four weeks later, final account: €38,500 in extra work. Answer: "Never approved. Show me the document." There is a three-word text message and an invoice drawn up after the fact.

01 / THE PROBLEM

Why this goes wrong so often.

A

The agreement is put on paper too late

The extra work is approved on site and only formalised days later — or never. What you then have is a one-sided document drawn up after the fact.

B

Whoever keeps the record is a party

Your site report comes from your system, which you can adjust yourself. For the court, that counts as "your version", not as fact.

C

With a fixed price, you bear the risk

Under a fixed-price construction contract, you can in principle only charge extra for additional work if the client agreed to it in writing. Without that proof, you are left with the bill.

02 / TODAY

What you can do now — even without Vidimus.

  • Work with an extra work order that the client signs on the spot: description, amount, date, two signatures. No order, no work.
  • Confirm a verbal agreement by email the same day with description and amount, and ask for a reply. A confirmation that is not disputed carries weight.
  • Photograph the situation before and after, with the date, and link the photos to the order.
  • Include an evidence clause in your construction contract: which records count as proof between the parties, without prejudice to the right to prove the contrary (Art. 8.2 of the Belgian Civil Code).
03 / THE LAW

What the law says.

Art. 1793 of the old Belgian Civil Code

Under a fixed-price construction contract, the contractor cannot charge extra for changes or additions unless they were authorised in writing and the price was agreed with the client. Courts sometimes read this more flexibly if the agreement is established in another way — but the risk lies with you.

Art. 8.11 of the Belgian Civil Code

Between businesses, evidence may be given by any means: emails, photos, a system's history and digital agreements are admissible — the court weighs them. The more independent the evidence, the more weight it carries.

Art. 8.2 of the Belgian Civil Code

Parties can agree in advance which records count as proof between them, without prejudice to the right to prove the contrary. Such a clause may not unreasonably limit the other party's means of evidence (Code of Economic Law, Art. VI.91/5, 7°). An evidence clause turns an anchored extra work order into an agreed means of proof.

General information, not legal advice. Vidimus provides evidence; how much weight it carries is for the court to assess — and that weight is exactly what anchoring increases.

05 / QUESTIONS

FAQ.

Is a text message or WhatsApp message not proof?

It is admissible evidence (between businesses, evidence may be given by any means), but it is thin: no amount, no description, and the counterparty often disputes the context. A signed, anchored order with amount and moment carries fundamentally more weight.

Does the client have to install software?

No. They sign on your phone or via a link; they receive the order and the verification link themselves. Nothing to install.

What if the client is a private individual?

Then stricter rules of evidence apply towards the consumer (above €3,500, a written document is in principle required). An order signed electronically by both parties is a written document — that is exactly the point.
