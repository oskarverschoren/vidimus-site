COUNTER-ARGUMENTS

Devil's advocate.

The counterparty checks the same data, but in a different order — does the hash not differ then?

No: before hashing, we bring every record into a canonical form (keys sorted, no whitespace, fixed encoding). After that, order no longer exists; the same content always gives the same bytes. That rule is in every bundle, with its name and version, and in the verification script. For files (photo, pdf) we hash the bytes themselves — there is no order to change there.

Do you have a copy of our data "as it was"? Otherwise there is nothing to compare.

Yes. On recording, the original (the canonical record and every attachment) goes encrypted into a write-once vault, for the retention period of the logbook. In case of doubt, the expert compares with what we received at the time — not with what your system exports today. And because the hash is on the ledger, we cannot change that copy unnoticed either.

Since last year, our system stores numbers differently (4.20 → 4.2). Then the data is "actually right" but the hash differs.

A hash that differs does not show that Vidimus failed, nor that the data was "actually right after all" — it proves that what is presented now is not byte for byte what was recorded at the time. What was recorded at the time is in the vault and in your bundle, with the timestamps of its group. That difference is not a problem: it is exactly the finding you want to be able to make. In practice: send the raw message from your source system, not an edited version.

What if Vidimus no longer exists in five years?

Your bundles then remain just as verifiable: standard cryptography (SHA-256, Ed25519, RFC 3161, Merkle), the verification script is included, and the timestamp file (.tsr) of the qualified trust service can be checked without Vidimus. Only the app disappears; the proof does not. That is why we deliver a bundle, not a screenshot.

Who has the keys?

Whoever signs has their own key — on their own device (browser/phone), not with us; we only see the public half and issue a certificate. Systems (TMS, gate) get a system key; a measurement source (sensor, logger) gets its own key, and only that source may measure. The original in the vault is encrypted per document; deleting after the retention period means destroying the key — the file becomes noise, and the fingerprint still proves that something was there.
