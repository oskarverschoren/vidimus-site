VIDIMUS/CASE FILES/SYNDICS & REAL ESTATE

Damage at move-out, "that was already there".

Damaged parquet, cracked tiles, a door that no longer closes. The tenant: "that was already there at move-in." Your photos: "taken after the key handover," he says. This page explains why the inspection report so often falls short, what the law requires, and how you make it verifiable for both parties — at move-in and at move-out.

Apartment, four rooms. Inspection report at move-in: signed, 38 photos on the property manager's laptop. Three years later, move-out: deep scratches in the living room parquet. The tenant disputes the date of the photos and the version of the document — "I signed a different version." Two PDFs, two truths.

01 / THE PROBLEM

Why this so often goes wrong.

A

The document lives in one system

The inspection report and the photos are on one laptop or in one property management package. Whoever manages it can change it — and so it gets disputed.

B

Photos without an independent moment

A photo has metadata, but it is trivial to change. Without an independent time, a photo does not prove when it was taken.

C

Years later, "the version" is unclear

After three years and two changes of property manager, it is no longer possible to reconstruct exactly which version was signed.

02 / TODAY

What you can already do now — even without Vidimus.

  • Make the inspection report detailed and drawn up jointly: together, room by room, with the meter readings, and dated and signed by both parties — before the key handover.
  • Give the tenant a copy immediately (email with attachment) and keep the proof of sending.
  • Number the photos, list them in the document and keep them in one place where they do not disappear "by accident".
  • Register the inspection report together with the lease (in Belgium, a lease must be officially registered) — this is mandatory and proves that it existed on that date.
03 / THE LAW

What the law says.

Art. 1730–1731 old Belgian Civil Code / Flemish Housing Rental Decree, Art. 9

A detailed inspection report at move-in is mandatory, drawn up jointly by both parties and at the expense of both. Without an inspection report, the tenant is presumed to have received the property in the state it is in at move-out — you then have to prove otherwise.

Art. 8.11 / 8.8 of the Belgian Civil Code

Between businesses, there is freedom of evidence; against a private tenant, stricter rules apply — a document signed by both parties is then your strongest piece of evidence. The easier the version and date are to check, the less room for dispute.

General information, not legal advice. Vidimus provides evidence; how much weight it carries is for the court to assess — and that weight is exactly what anchoring increases.

04 / ANCHORED

What changes.

  • At move-in, the inspection report is signed by both parties and hashed at that moment — including the fingerprint of every photo — and anchored within five minutes. For customer organisations, the group it is in usually also gets a qualified timestamp within six hours from GLOBALTRUST, a qualified trust service provider on the EU trusted list; the inspection report is linked to it through its Merkle path.
  • At move-out, years later, the document is still byte for byte exactly what was signed; any other "version" is exposed.
  • The tenant receives the verification link himself: he does not have to believe you, he can check it. That takes the sting out of the conversation before it becomes a dispute.

05 / QUESTIONS

FAQ.

May an inspection report be signed digitally?

Yes. An electronic signature may not be refused solely because it is electronic (eIDAS Art. 25). For residential leases, we advise also registering the document along with the lease, as the law requires.

What about the photos — do they have to go to your cloud?

No. A fingerprint of every photo is anchored; the photo itself stays with you (or encrypted with us, if you want). The photo cannot be reconstructed from the fingerprint.

Can we see move-in and move-out side by side?

Yes. The dashboard puts two records with the same reference side by side: which fields changed, and the photos per room (kitchen, bathroom, meter readings) — with the caption typed in at the moment itself. Each side remains its own record with its own fingerprint, signature and timestamp; the comparison changes nothing.

Does this also work for a syndic, for the common areas?

Yes — the same method works for a syndic (the managing agent of a building in co-ownership in Belgium): handovers of common areas, key handovers and work by contractors in the building.
