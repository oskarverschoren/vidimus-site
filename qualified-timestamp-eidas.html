VIDIMUS/QUESTIONS/TIMESTAMP AND EIDAS

What does a qualified timestamp (eIDAS) prove for your business documents?

Short answerA qualified electronic timestamp comes from a service on the EU trusted list. Under eIDAS, a legal presumption applies to it throughout the EU: that the date and time are accurate and that the data it is bound to have not changed since. It does not say who made the document, nor whether the content is true.

01 / HOW IT WORKS

How it works.

  1. A fingerprint of the documentsha-256

    A fingerprint is calculated from the document. Only that fingerprint goes to the timestamping service, not the document itself.

  2. The service signs fingerprint and timerfc 3161

    The timestamping service signs the fingerprint together with the time on its clock. The result is a small timestamp file (.tsr). It is qualified only if the service is listed as a qualified timestamping service on the EU trusted list.

  3. Recompute lateropenssl · DSS · trusted list

    Whoever receives the document recalculates the fingerprint, checks the timestamp file (with openssl or with DSS, the European Commission's software) and looks up the service in the EU trusted list.

  4. At Vidimus: per groupmerkle tree · globaltrust

    Vidimus combines five minutes of fingerprints into a Merkle tree. The root of that group gets a timestamp from an external service (Sectigo) within five minutes and then an anchor in Bitcoin. Groups with records of customer organisations usually also get a qualified timestamp from GLOBALTRUST within six hours, as long as our annual package with GLOBALTRUST has room left. Your record is linked to it through its Merkle path, which you recompute yourself.

  5. In the evidence bundleevidence record · rfc 4998

    The bundle contains both timestamp files, the Merkle path, the Bitcoin proof and an Evidence Record (RFC 4998) for DSS. For our demo record, DSS gave the outcome PASSED: a technical check, not an approval or certification of Vidimus.

02 / THE LAW

What the law says.

eIDAS, Art. 41(1)

An electronic timestamp may not be refused as evidence solely because it is electronic or not qualified. So an ordinary timestamp also counts; the court weighs it freely.

eIDAS, Art. 41(2)

For a qualified timestamp, there is a presumption that the date and time are accurate and that the data it is bound to are unchanged. At Vidimus, that timestamp is on the group (the Merkle root); whether the presumption also applies to your record is for the court to assess.

eIDAS, Art. 41(3)

A qualified timestamp issued in one member state is recognised as a qualified timestamp in every other member state.

Art. 8.11 of the Belgian Civil Code

Between businesses, there is freedom of evidence: all means of evidence are admissible and the court weighs them.

Articles summarised, not quoted. General information, not legal advice.

03 / LIMITS

What it does not prove.

  • Who made or sent the document. That is what a signature is for. At Vidimus, the device or system signs with its own key (Ed25519); that is not a qualified signature.
  • That the content is true. A wrong invoice stays wrong, but is recorded as 'this existed then'.
  • That the document did not exist earlier. The timestamp says: no later than this moment.
  • At Vidimus: a separate qualified timestamp per record. It is on the group, and only for groups with records of customer organisations. Internal and demo organisations get the ordinary timestamp, which is not qualified.
  • That Vidimus itself is a qualified service. The qualified timestamp comes from GLOBALTRUST, not from Vidimus.

05 / QUESTIONS

Frequently asked questions.

How can you see whether a timestamp is qualified?

Open the timestamp file (.tsr) or the bundle in DSS, the European Commission's software. If the timestamp comes from a qualified service, the detailed report shows it. You can also look up the service yourself in the EU trusted list.

Is an ordinary timestamp worthless, then?

No. An electronic timestamp may not be refused as evidence solely because it is not qualified. It just does not give the same legal presumption; the court weighs it freely.

Does every record at Vidimus get its own qualified timestamp?

No. The qualified timestamp is on the group (the five-minute Merkle root), and only groups with records of customer organisations get one, usually within six hours. Your record is linked to it through its Merkle path. Every group does get an ordinary timestamp within five minutes and then an anchor in Bitcoin.

Do you still need a notary for business documents?

For most documents between businesses, no: there is freedom of evidence and a timestamp is a means of evidence. Where the law requires an authentic instrument (a deed drawn up by a notary), the notary remains necessary; a timestamp does not replace it.

Does the qualified timestamp cost extra?

No. It is included in the price per record; the first 100 records a month are free.
