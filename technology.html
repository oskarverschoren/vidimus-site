How the proof works.

For anyone who wants to know exactly what happens: what is recorded, how you check it yourself, largely outside Vidimus, and what Vidimus does and does not prove.

Why your database cannot do this.

A lot of business software keeps a detailed history within its own environment (an audit trail): who, what, when, sometimes with the value before and after. That is useful. But that history sits in the same system as the data. It is not the same as anchoring a single record outside that system, which a third party recomputes for themselves.

Your systems remain the source. Vidimus adds a proof layer outside your system to the records that matter to you: a delivery, a measurement, an inspection, a photo, an agreement. The time with evidential value does not come from us.

In three steps

No migration, no replacement. One thin layer on top of what you already have.

Connect

01
Source: your TMS or ERP, via API or webhook

Your system forwards each record. No system? Then the app: photo, location and signature, with a phone and a QR code, without installation. Vidimus scans and installs nothing at the customer's site: the integration is built by your vendor, your IT team or your own AI assistant. The only thing Vidimus fetches is a web address that you provide yourself to be recorded.

Anchor

02
Anchor: every five minutes, outside your system

Fingerprint and signature, anchored per five-minute group with a timestamp; groups with customer records usually also receive the qualified timestamp from GLOBALTRUST within six hours. Then into a ledger in which each link refers to the previous one: removing or changing something stands out, even if we were to do it.

Check

03
Check: public, without an account

The counterparty, an insurer, a judge, even years later. On the check page, or with the evidence bundle and its check script; the bundle remains verifiable if our platform no longer exists.

What cannot happen unnoticed, not even by us

  • Giving an earlier time or changing it afterwardsNobody can give a record an earlier anchoring time, not even us. The time in the record itself is what the device reported. The anchoring time comes from an external timestamping service and from a Bitcoin block, not from our clock. One letter different and the check shows it.
  • Signing in your name, if you hold the key yourselfWhoever signs does so with their own key: on the phone, or for your system through our SDK. If your system holds the key itself, we never see it. If you let us keep the key, that is stated in every bundle.
  • Reading your contentEverything is stored encrypted (AES‑256). If our server opens your content, for example for an evidence bundle, that shows up in your access log. Choose a confidential logbook or let your system encrypt the content itself: then the key is only with you, and Vidimus sees the fingerprint and a short envelope (type, reference, source and time), not the content. The proof remains verifiable; Vidimus then cannot show the content.

What Vidimus proves. And what it does not.

The limit, honestly

Vidimus shows: this is the record that was recorded at that moment, and a change to that content stands out afterwards. Not that the driver really delivered 18 pallets, or that the sensor was properly calibrated. The closer to the moment something is recorded and the more parties co-sign, the harder lying becomes. Who is right is for the court to decide.

Proves
  • That this content existed no later than that moment
  • Through which source, which device and which organisation it was recorded
  • That it has not changed since
  • That the counterparty co-signed, if they did
Does not prove
  • That the content is true
  • That a sensor measures accurately
  • Which change was made afterwards

Show the full specification
WhatTechnologyExplanation
whatSHA-256

Fingerprint over the canonical JSON of the record, including the hashes of every attachment, so the photos too. For logbooks with personal data, a secret salt for that one record is included.

whoEd25519 + X.509

Device keys, created on the phone; anyone who turns on Face ID or a passkey adds, with every signature, a second proof that the user verified themselves on the device. Systems sign with their own system key. Every key has an X.509 certificate under the Vidimus certificate chain; the name in a device certificate is supplied by the employer, not verified by Vidimus.

whenRFC 3161 · eIDAS QTSP

A timestamp from Sectigo within about 5 minutes. Groups with records from customer organisations usually also receive a qualified timestamp from GLOBALTRUST within 6 hours; your record is linked to it through its Merkle path. Our server clock does not determine the moment.

whereGPS + accuracy

Position and margin of error as the device reports them, as part of the hashed content. We do not measure; we record what the device said.

bundledMerkle root

One root per five-minute group; each anchor proves its own place in the group without revealing the other records.

chainLedger, append-only

Each entry hashes the previous one. Change one entry and the chain no longer matches from that point on, which you can check with verify_chain.

publicOpenTimestamps → Bitcoin

Every root also goes to OpenTimestamps and is included in a Bitcoin block within a few hours: a public register that thousands of parties keep at the same time. Changing or deleting stands out to anyone who checks, even if we were to try it. The proof (.ots) can be recomputed against Bitcoin itself, without Vidimus.

checkEvidence bundle

Check page plus a bundle with a check script: recomputable without Vidimus, largely offline. Content stays in the EU; only fingerprints go to the timestamping services and OpenTimestamps.

Two witnesses outside Vidimus: the timestamp (and, for customer organisations, the qualified timestamp from GLOBALTRUST) and Bitcoin via OpenTimestamps. For you, nothing changes: no crypto, no wallet, no consortium. You check the proof both inside and outside Vidimus: on the check page, or with the bundle and open tools.
